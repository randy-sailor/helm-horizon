The Dealer Tier Gets an Infrastructure Owner

What $53 a share actually buys

On 9 August MarineMax signed an agreement and plan of merger with affiliates of Safe Harbor Marinas, a portfolio company of Blackstone Infrastructure Partners, at $53.00 a share in cash for an enterprise value of roughly $1.5 billion (MarineMax 8-K). The price was a 96 per cent premium to the 30 January close of $27.03, the last trading day before an unsolicited approach became public (Tampa Bay Business & Wealth). The process has now substantially cleared: the Hart-Scott-Rodino waiting period expired at the end of October, foreign antitrust and foreign direct investment clearances were received, and shareholders approved the transaction at a special meeting on 11 November, with closing expected by the end of calendar 2026 (MarineMax 10-K).

The company being bought is smaller than it was and better than it was. Fiscal 2026 revenue fell 6.3 per cent to $2.164 billion, gross profit rose 0.5 per cent to $754.2 million, and gross margin reached 34.9 per cent against 32.5 per cent a year earlier (MarineMax 10-K). Fourth-quarter revenue was $520.1 million with same-store sales down 5.7 per cent and gross margin up 270 basis points to 37.4 per cent; inventories ended the year at $812.8 million, down $54.6 million, after a $39.3 million reduction the year before (MarineMax 8-K). Management declined to give fiscal 2027 guidance or hold a call, which is normal practice into a close and also means the channel loses its most useful quarterly read on premium retail.

Three action steps

What to do this quarter

1

Run the MarineMax playbook on your own P&L before year-end.

MarineMax did not beat because boats sold — same-store sales were down 7%. It beat because brokerage, finance and insurance, marinas, superyachts, and parts and service carried the gross profit. Break out your last-twelve-month gross profit by revenue line and mark the non-new-boat share. If it is below 40%, you are running a 2022 business model in a 2026 market.

2

Move charter cross-currency exposure to the front of the risk register.

With EU diesel back at €1.93 a litre and marine gasoil up 73.5% since February, a euro-priced Med charter against a dollar cost book is materially different from what your advance provisioning allowance assumed in April. Requote with a fresh fuel assumption, a 300–500 basis point currency buffer, and an explicit war-risk surcharge pass-through clause.

3

Book Monaco with a builder-services agenda, not a boat-shopping agenda.

The Monaco Yacht Show runs 23–26 September with roughly 120 yachts and 43 new deliveries expected — but the real story on the pontoons will be builder distress. Arrive with a list: which builders hold open orders on your clients’ hulls, which yards carry credible completion risk, and which service providers you can lock in before demand snaps back.
