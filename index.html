What $53.00 a share is actually buying

The arithmetic of the last reported quarter is worth setting out plainly, because it is the arithmetic the buyer underwrote. Revenue of $520.1 million was down from $552.2 million, with same-store sales off 5.7 per cent. Gross profit nonetheless rose to $194.3 million, or 37.4 per cent of revenue, from 34.7 per cent, which the company attributes to improved boat profitability and a greater contribution from higher-margin businesses. Selling, general and administrative expense ran at 37.7 per cent of revenue on a reported basis — inflated by transaction costs — but adjusted selling, general and administrative expense rose only 1.8 per cent to $181.1 million. Interest expense fell to $13.2 million from $17.3 million. Adjusted EBITDA rose 26 per cent to $21.8 million on falling revenue. Inventories closed the year at $812.8 million, down $54.6 million or 6.3 per cent, following a $39.3 million reduction the year before (MarineMax Form 8-K).

For the full year, revenue was $2.2 billion, same-store sales fell 5.5 per cent, gross margin rose 240 basis points to 34.9 per cent, and adjusted EBITDA was $112.6 million against a reported net loss of $8.2 million. Management's own framing is the useful one: gross margin has gone from 26.1 per cent in fiscal 2019 to 34.9 per cent in fiscal 2026 on the back of the non-boat businesses — more than 65 marina and storage facilities, IGY Marinas, Fraser Yachts Group and Northrop & Johnson in superyacht brokerage and services, Cruisers Yachts and Intrepid in manufacturing, plus finance and insurance. Safe Harbor is not buying a dealer group that owns some marinas. It is buying a marina and superyacht services platform that happens to retain more than 70 dealerships.

Three action steps

What to do this quarter

1

Run the MarineMax playbook on your own P&L before year-end.

MarineMax did not beat because boats sold — same-store sales were down 7%. It beat because brokerage, finance and insurance, marinas, superyachts, and parts and service carried the gross profit. Break out your last-twelve-month gross profit by revenue line and mark the non-new-boat share. If it is below 40%, you are running a 2022 business model in a 2026 market.

2

Move charter cross-currency exposure to the front of the risk register.

With EU diesel back at €1.93 a litre and marine gasoil up 73.5% since February, a euro-priced Med charter against a dollar cost book is materially different from what your advance provisioning allowance assumed in April. Requote with a fresh fuel assumption, a 300–500 basis point currency buffer, and an explicit war-risk surcharge pass-through clause.

3

Book Monaco with a builder-services agenda, not a boat-shopping agenda.

The Monaco Yacht Show runs 23–26 September with roughly 120 yachts and 43 new deliveries expected — but the real story on the pontoons will be builder distress. Arrive with a list: which builders hold open orders on your clients’ hulls, which yards carry credible completion risk, and which service providers you can lock in before demand snaps back.
